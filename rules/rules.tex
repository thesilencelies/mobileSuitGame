\section{Welcome to NetFrame}
In NetFrame you command a team of Frames, 10 meter tall fighting robots that amplify their pilots connection to the
reality warping power of the Net. You'll fight other factions Frames to try to gain 
control of the districts of the city that extends as far as anyone knows and leave your 
mark on this world where the digital is real.


These rules set out a method of handling movement and combat between Frames. The game is played across 5 turns, after which the team with the most victory points wins.
Each player brings the same number of Frames, each with their own deck. A good default number is 3 per player, although with teams 2 each can be smoother.
 With many players teams are recommended over battle royale, though the rules support either.
All frames a player brings should be from the same faction.

\section{Victory Points}
Victory points are obtained in two key ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item 1 point per enemy frame defeated 
\item points equal to the amount written on the objective for each objective you score. 
\end{itemize}

Objectives are terrain cards with two points numbers on the top left, and a text box that describes how the objective can be scored.
The defending player is the player who brought that objective card, the other player is the attacker. The green score is what the defender
scores, the red is what the attacker scores if they score the objective.


\section{The Battlefield}

Terrain blocks movement and line of sight. Terrain that can be stood upon is classified as being on different elevations. 

\subsection{Terrain Cards}
Each player must bring a terrain deck of 10 cards, and an objective deck of 5 cards.
Objectives (terrain cards that indicate objective scores) can only be in the objectives deck.
These cards are shuffled, dealt and construct the battlefield. On the card is a grid, on which the frames move and ranges are reckoned. 
The border of the tiles indicate the elevation.

\begin{tabular}{c c}
black & ground/elevation 0 \\
dark blue & elevation 1 \\
blue & elevation 2 \\ 
light blue & elevation 3\\
red & impassable \\
\end{tabular}

Tiles that contain obstacles have a yellow dashing on their border and yellow diagonal lines.
Tiles that indicate objectives have green lines.
Tiles where a token should be placed on them (explained in the rules text on the card) have orange hatching.

Movement on these tiles is lateral and diagonal. The 8 tiles surrounding any one tile are all adjacent to it (so would count for melee attacks).
Ranges are considered the same way (so you can consider the maximum range a square centered on the frame with sides of length twice the range + 1).
Movement is counted in steps.

\section{Setup}
Each player builds a grid of 2 rows of (number of frames + 2) from their terrain deck.
(for 2 players with 3 frames each that makes a 5x4 grid).
First, each player takes one objective card for each of their rows and chooses where to place it within that row --- one objective per row.
The remaining slots in the grid are then filled by dealing out the rest of the terrain cards.
Any relevant tokens are placed on the terrain cards.
All objectives on these terrain cards are valid for this battle - you are defending any objectives that are on your team's terrain cards.

For each frame the players choose their starting hand of 7, shuffle the deck then put those 7 on top.

Each player takes it in turns to put one of their frames on nearest edge of their terrain cards.
After all frames are deployed this way the player who deployed first receives the priority marker.

\twocolumn[{\centering\LARGE\textbf{The Turn}\par\medskip}]
On each turn of the game there are 3 phases: planning, action and cleanup. 

\section{Planning}
In the planning phase, players simultaneously choose actions for their frames one frame at a time.
To chose actions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Draw 7 cards from that frame's deck (modified by status)
  \item Place 2 cards face down - those are the chosen actions
  \item Put the rest of the cards in that frame's discard pile
\end{itemize}
If you would draw from an empty deck, shuffle that deck's discard pile and it becomes the deck.

Once all actions are chosen for all frames the planning phase ends.

\subsection{Echoes of the fallen}
If a frame has been defeated, at the end of the planning phase the player can reveal the top card of that frame's deck and put it sideways with the actions of a surviving frame. That card can also be used to block attacks against that frame this turn (ignore the rest of the card text).

\subsection{Status update}
At the end of the planning phase, one status token of each type is removed from each frame.


\section{Action}
Cards are resolved in order of the initiative value, starting with the highest.

To work out which card has the highest initiative left, each player declares the highest remaining initiative amongst their remaining cards at the start of the phase and after each card resolution.
In the event of a tie the cards are resolved alternately, moving clockwise from the player with the priority marker.

Card resolution consists of 3 steps: movement, effect, and attack, in any order. You cannot split the movement up.

\subsection{Movement}
The frame can move up to their base movement plus the movement modifier on the card currently being resolved steps in any direction.

\subsubsection{Movement penalties}
It takes 1 movement to move up per elevation difference, and you cannot stop halfway up. It costs no additional movement to move down any number of elevations. 
Obstacles and impassible terrain cannot be moved through.

\subsection{Effect}
After movement is done, any effect on the card is applied. Effects are applied in printed order.

\subsection{Attack}
If the action has any attack listed, one frame within any of the ranges may be chosen as a target.
Attacks without a listed range are melee attacks, and the target must be adjacent.
Else the attack is a ranged attack and the target cannot be adjacent.
If an attack has multiple ranges, only the zones that are in range count for the attack.

\subsubsection{Line of sight}
To select a target for a ranged attack the target must be:
\begin{itemize}
\item within the range of the attack
\item not adjacent to the attacking frame
\end{itemize}
Then draw a line starting from anywhere on the the attacking frame to the center of the target.
If there are no obstructions on that line, the target is valid.

The following count as an obstruction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Impassable terrain
  \item Terrain at a higher elevation than the attacker
  \item Obstacles adjacent to the target
  \item Terrain at a higher elevation than the target adjacent to the target
\end{itemize}

A tile with a frame on it counts as one elevation higher when checking line of sight. For example a frame standing on flat ground obstructs like an elevation-1 tile.

In the figures below, \textcolor{yellow!75!black}{\textbf{A}} is the attacking frame and the purple discs are possible targets: a \textcolor{green!45!black}{green tick} marks a target in line of sight, a \textcolor{red!80!black}{red cross} one that is not. A red \textcolor{red!85!black}{$\otimes$} marks whatever breaks the line.

attack a single matching zone stops the whole thing; against \guardbreak\ each zone must be
blocked on its own, and any zone left uncovered still deals its damage.
A blocking card covers \emph{every} attacked zone it has a block in --- one card, spent once.

Blocking stays compulsory while any remaining card blocks any zone that is still unblocked,
and the defender chooses which card to spend each time --- so the order matters.
Against a \guardbreak\ attack on all three zones, a defender holding one card that blocks all
three and another that blocks only Low spends a single card by blocking with the wide one
first, but two cards if they spend the Low block first, since High and Mid are then still open
and must still be blocked.

\subsubsection{Melee between elevations}
If the attack is a melee attack and the defender is at a different elevation to the attacker, the attacks are moved up one bracket per difference in elevation.
Any attacks that are moved higher than High or lower than Low are considered out of range.

So for example, Frame A at elevation 1 attacks Frame B at elevation 3 with cleave, which has \smallcutx{2} high and \smallcutx{2} mid. The attacker is 2 elevations lower than the defender, so the attacks are moved 2 stages down.
The \smallcutx{2} high end up \smallcutx{2} low, and the \smallcutx{2} mid are out of range.
Conversely if Frame C at elevation 2 were to attack Frame A with Thrust (\smallpierce mid), it would be moved one stage up, resulting in \smallpierce high. This would be blocked by a high block, not a mid block.

Ranged attacks are not affected by elevation in this way.


\subsubsection{Damage}
If a frame takes more damage in any zone than the number indicated on the frame card for that zone they are destroyed.
Destroyed frames are removed from the battlefield and can no longer contribute to the fight. Their remaining actions and abilities are discarded.
Damage persists unless repaired using special frame actions.
Additionally any frame that has one hit left in any zone faces the following penalty corresponding to the zone: 

\begin{tabular}{c p{4cm}}
High &
-1 initiative (reduce initiative on all actions by 1) \\
Mid &
-1 cards (draw one less card to choose actions from) \\
Low &
-1 movement (reduce base movement by 1) \\
\end{tabular}
For example a \emph{Kuwagata} ( Armor 4 4 4) that has taken 3 damage high, 3 damage mid and 1 low draws 6 cards a turn and it's actions resolve
at -1 initiative. If it took one more damage high or mid, or 3 more damage low it would be destroyed. 



\section{Cleanup}
Cards without a persistence marker are put into their respective discard piles.
Cards with a persistence marker that are still active are set aside clearly distinct from the turn's chosen actions - 
they do not resolve again or block, they exist only in conjunction with the card effect.

Cards with a persistence marker who's effect has ended (for example a card that says Next Turn: that resolved last turn) are also discarded.


The priority marker then moves one step anticlockwise.

\section{Tokens}
Terrain and various card effects create tokens. These can normally be indicated by anything you have around, 
coins, dice, whatever, as long as it is clear to everyone what it is. Dedicated token pieces are also manufactured
for players who wish to use them. 

If a token has a specified health stat, then it can be attacked by an opposing team the same way as a frame can be.
Tokens never block, and they only have one health stat, so all damage from all zones affects its health the same.
If it takes damage past its health stat, it is destroyed. Unless a card says otherwise, tokens cannot be repaired.

\subsection{Drones}
Some attacks summon drones. A drone is a token that will take the action on the card that summoned it every turn
(including the turn that it is summoned), except that it moves with its own movement value (the one next to its health bar).
The drone has a health stat indicated on the card that summoned it, which should stay in play until the drone is destroyed.
The drone, like other tokens, cannot block.



\section{Status}
Various cards apply certain status effects to frames. These status effects are marked with a counter added to the frame.
As long as the frame has any quantity of a status counter on it, the frame's stats are modified according to the status.
The modification amount is the same regardless of the quantity of status counters.

\begin{tabular}{p{4cm}| p{4cm}}
Debuff & Buff \\
\fullstunned & \fullstimmed \\
\fulldazed  & \fulllucid \\
\fullslowed & \fullboosted \\
\fullrevealed & \\
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Status annihilation}
If a frame would receives the opposite status to the status it already has,
the counters cancel each other out, leaving the difference. For example, if a frame with \slowed gets 2 \boosted 
\subsection{Status application}
The number of status counters on the frame indicate the duration of the effect.
Each turn, after the planning phase finishes one of each status counter is removed.



\section{Deck Construction}
Each frame must have a deck.
To construct a deck, you first choose the frame.
That frame has a stats card with the following information:


\begin{itemize}
\item Base movement
\item Faction
\item Armor values at each zone
\item Deck size \includegraphics[width=0.4cm]{../icons/deckImg.png}
\item Weapon count \includegraphics[width=0.4cm]{../icons/weapon.png}
\item Booster count \includegraphics[width=0.4cm]{../icons/boosterImg.png}
\item Frame ability (if any)
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[!ht]
  \centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../pictures/ground_zero.png}
\end{figure}

The frame's deck must have exactly the number of cards in its deck size count in it.
To build the deck, you can have:

\begin{itemize}
\item Max 4 pilot cards, all from the same pilot, no duplicates
\item Max X booster cards, where X is the frame's booster count (duplicates allowed)
\item Up to N weapons where N is the frame's weapon count. Each weapon is a set of up to 4 attack cards.
 For each weapon slot you can include up to 1 of each card in that weapon.
 Drones take up a weapon slot
\item Any number of the basic cards
\item 1 of each of the frame cards for your faction 
\end{itemize}

If any card has a faction logo on it, it can only be in a deck for a frame of that faction.

  Committed & This attack is discarded after it resolves \\

\section{Optional variations}

\subsection{Prebuilt Terrain}
Instead of using the terrain cards you can use whatever terrain setup you have at your gaming table. 
Players should quickly agree at the start of the game what elevation each piece of terrain is,
but as a quick rule of thumb, moving up one frame's height is roughly one elevation.
In this case, for objectives you can either draw objective cards from the deck and place appropriate markers and tokens at a location of your choosing, 
or use pre agreed objectives and the objectives on the cards can be used as reference.
Distances are measured with a tape measure and a fixed base unit should be established based on the scale of the miniatures and terrain 
(for example, 1" is one step is about correct for 28mm miniatures). 
Deployment zones are 7 units away from the center line.



\section{Tips}
Here are some pointers to help you get started in your battles in NetFrame.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Heavy attacks root you in place.} If you intend to swing something big, be
where you need to be already, or pair it with a booster.
\item \textbf{Ranged attacks pay more movement.} Range is not free; gunners
tend to have to stand still to shoot.

\item \textbf{Attacking is how you spend your opponent's cards.} Blocking is compulsory
and the blocking card is discarded, so every blocked attack strips a card from them.
\item \textbf{A fast attack can delete a slow heavy one.} If their low initiative
attack is still face down when your attack forces a block, they may have to
expend it so it never resolves at all. This is what attacks with \feint are for.
\item \textbf{High initiative is defensive gold} Resolved attacks hang around to block after
so they can both remove an opponents action and then block as well.
\item \textbf{Most weapons cannot block every zone.} Many cover only one. Attack the zone
their weapon leaves open.
\item \textbf{Watch the group indicator} at the bottom left of every weapon card. It shows
what the whole weapon group can do. One card played tells you some of whats coming.
\item \textbf{Almost every ranged attack blocks Mid, and only Mid.} A frame with only
guns has a very predictable guard: hit it High or Low.
\item \textbf{Deck size trades variety for consistency.} A small deck sees a large share of
itself every turn and can rely on finding its key cards; a large one carries far more
tools and finds any particular one far less often.
\item \textbf{Cover your own blocking zones.} Check which zones your chosen weapons leave
open and fill the gap - everyone has access to the basic attacks.
\end{itemize}

\qhead{Turn structure}
Each turn: \textbf{Planning} $\to$ \textbf{Action} $\to$ \textbf{Cleanup}.
\begin{qreflist}
\item \textbf{Planning} (simultaneous), per frame:
\begin{qreflist}
  \item  draw 7 ($\pm$ status)
  \item choose 2 face-down
  \item discard others
  \item remove one of each status counter.
\end{qreflist}
\item \textbf{Action}: resolve by initiative, highest first.
  \begin{qreflist}
  \item ties: alternate clockwise from the priority marker;
  \item each card $=$ Movement, Effect, Attack in any order.
  \end{qreflist}
\item \textbf{Cleanup}: discard non-persistent cards; set active persistent cards aside.
  \begin{qreflist}\item priority marker $\to$ one step anticlockwise.\end{qreflist}
\end{qreflist}

\qhead{Movement}
\begin{qreflist}
\item Base movement $+$ card modifier, any direction.
\item Climb: 1 extra per elevation, no stopping mid-climb.
\item Descend: free.
\item Cannot cross obstacles or impassable tiles.
\end{qreflist}

\qhead{Attack}
\begin{qreflist}
\item One target in range:
  \begin{qreflist}
  \item melee (no range) $\to$ adjacent;
  \item ranged $\to$ not adjacent $+$ line of sight.
  \end{qreflist}
\item Multi-range attack: only in-range zones count.
\item Defender \textbf{must} block if a remaining card can.
\item Unblocked: 1 damage per mark; attacker's card stays out (can block later).
\item Melee vs elevation: shift zones 1 per level (lower $\to$ down, higher $\to$ up); off High/Low $=$ out of range. Ranged unaffected.
\end{qreflist}

\qhead{Blocking}
\begin{qreflist}
\item \textbf{Compulsory}: if a remaining card blocks \emph{any} attacked zone, you must block.
\item One block stops the \emph{whole} attack; every zone, even ones it doesn't cover.
\item The blocking card is discarded afterwards (a \emph{super block} is kept).
\end{qreflist}

\qhead{Damage}
\begin{qreflist}
\item More damage in a zone than its armour $\to$ frame destroyed.
\item Last hit left in a zone gives a standing penalty:
  \begin{qreflist}
  \item High $\to -1$ initiative;
  \item Mid $\to -1$ card drawn;
  \item Low $\to -1$ movement.
  \end{qreflist}
\end{qreflist}

\qhead{Line of sight (ranged)}
\begin{qreflist}
\item Target: in range, not adjacent.
\item Clear line attacker $\to$ target $=$ valid.
\item Blockers on the line:
  \begin{qreflist}
  \item impassable terrain;
  \item terrain higher than the attacker;
  \item an obstacle next to the target;
  \item higher terrain next to the target.
  \end{qreflist}
\item A tile with a frame on it counts as \textbf{one elevation higher}.
\end{qreflist}

\qhead{Status effects}
Applies while any counter remains; opposite status cancels one-for-one.
